## Definition & Scope

> *What is this concept, and what does it include or exclude?*

Customer Success can often feel like an isolated endeavor within an organization.
Since Customer Success Managers are deeply involved in every aspect of the customer journey, and bear the weight of ensuring that customers not only use a product but also achieve their desired outcomes with it, it can often feel like a solo mission.
However, the reality is that aligning a product with customer needs and driving optimal outcomes requires a collaborative effort, particularly when it comes to aspects of the customer relationship that deal with the product itself.
For that, Customer Success teams can—and should—connect and collaborate with the Engineering team at their organization.
While Customer Success handles the face-to-face with customers, the Engineering team develops the software behind the product and is responsible for things like new features, bug patches, and product updates.
And the good news is that collaboration between Customer Success and the Engineering team can be mutually beneficial to both departments.
An effective relationship and excellent communication can help the entire organization build better products, roll out necessary upgrades and advancements, and help maximize the overall customer experience.
**This article will discuss:**
- Why Customer Success should connect with Engineering teams
- How Customer Success can collaborate with Engineering teams
- Steps for collaboration between Customer Success and the Engineering team
...
While they might appear to have different goals and day-to-day functions, Customer Success and the Engineering team shouldn’t operate completely independently of each other.
That’s because, overarchingly, both departments are working towards the same business goals, such as customer satisfaction, reduced churn, and improved revenue.
Instead of working completely separately, Customer Success and the Engineering team should work together to have a mutually beneficial relationship that can be leveraged on both sides of the equation.
…
Both Customer Success and the Engineering team can benefit from connecting and collaborating with one another.
Firstly, Customer Success can benefit from a close relationship with the Engineering team, because with this relationship, they’ll have a direct line to the product and platform experts.
That means if there’s an issue or need, Customer Success will know exactly who to reach in order to ensure it’s resolved as quickly as possible.
…
- **Align on goals and needs: ** To kick off a mutually beneficial relationship between Customer Success and the Engineering team, there should first be alignment on the goals of each department from the relationship, as well as what each side needs.
For example, maybe the goals of Customer Success are to find customer solutions more quickly and seamlessly, while the goals of the Engineering team are to decrease the time it takes to roll out urgent bug patches.
Goal alignment can help ensure that everyone is working toward the same outcomes.
…
- **Track and measure progress: ** Like all business efforts, this collaboration between Customer Success and Engineering should be tracked and measured.
To do so, leaders can connect this collaboration to specific KPIs, for example.
Tracking the efforts of this collaboration can help everyone understand the ROI and determine ways the effort can be improved.
Additionally, there can be a feedback system in place, where both Customer Success and the Engineering team are invited to provide feedback in a constructive and useful way.
Customer Success shouldn’t operate in a silo, and, instead, can benefit from relationships and collaboration with other departments, including the Engineering team.
But this isn’t just a one-sided relationship, and the Engineering team can also benefit from connecting with Customer Success, by gaining useful customer insights and details into the customer sentiment that can help shape the product.
By connecting with one another, Customer Success and the Engineering team can both contribute to better products and features and can work towards customer resolutions together.

## Fostering Cross-Functional Collaboration
Encourage open communication between engineering and other departments, such as sales, marketing, and customer success, to gain a deep understanding of customer challenges and pain points.
This collaboration can include regular meetings, shared documentation, or joint workshops.
Identifying the interdependence across different departments makes it easier to map the chain of values associated with the engineering team.
This promotes a holistic understanding of the &quot;why&quot; behind the product rather than getting limited to the &quot;how.&quot;
## Setting Customer-Driven Objectives
Translate customer feedback into actionable product requirements that directly address their needs.
Utilize goal-setting methodologies, such as Objectives and Key Results (OKRs), to ensure that objectives are customer-focused, measurable, and achievable.
The engineering team&#39;s goals should derive from the company&#39;s objectives and encompass more than just technical goals.
They should include initiatives that enable measuring the product&#39;s achievements, such as the adoption rate of a given feature or improving the accuracy of reports and models.
Interdependent goals help everyone understand what the company wants to achieve and how engineering contributes to those goals.
…
Promote strong cross-collaboration between engineering and customer-facing teams, such as Sales, Success, and Support.
These teams possess valuable information extracted from various channels, including surveys, interviews, product usage data, and personal experience.
Incorporate this information into your decision-making process!
Once you have gathered customer feedback, communicate it clearly to the engineering team, highlighting potential areas of improvement and success stories.
Empower engineers to analyze the feedback, identify patterns and trends, and facilitate interactive development.
This approach allows teams to quickly respond to customers&#39; needs and make the necessary changes to the product.

## Conclusion: Embracing a Customer-Centric Mindset
In conclusion, embracing a customer-centric mindset and succeeding in today&#39;s competitive market requires understanding that &quot;we are all products.&quot;
Regardless of their role, every team member must take ownership of the product&#39;s success and focus on delivering value to customers.
Engineering teams, in particular, play a critical role in developing innovative solutions that address customer needs.
By fostering cross-functional collaboration, setting customer-driven objectives, integrating customer feedback into the development process, and tracking relevant KPIs, engineering teams can thrive in creating products that resonate with customers and drive business success.
