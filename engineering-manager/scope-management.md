## Definition & Scope

> *What is this concept, and what does it include or exclude?*

### 1.
Plan your scope.
A well-defined scope management plan is the foundation of a successful project.
Start by gathering input from all key stakeholders to align objectives, priorities, and constraints.
This process helps establish how you will:
- **Handle unforeseen changes** — Define how to manage ad-hoc requests, reallocate resources, and adjust timelines when necessary.
- **Manage project deliverables** — Clarify roles and responsibilities for approving and processing completed work.
- **Define key scope components** — Develop essential documents such as the scope statement and work breakdown structure (WBS) to guide project execution.
…
### 6.
Monitor and control scope to prevent scope creep.
Changes can happen even with a well-defined scope, but they must be controlled.
Ongoing scope monitoring ensures that deviations from the original plan are identified and addressed before they become significant issues.
To minimize scope creep:
- Continuously track progress against the scope statement.
- Use performance reports to compare actual versus planned work.
- Ensure change requests go through a formal evaluation and approval process.

## Best practices for project scope management.
anchor
best-practices-for-project-scope-management
Here are the best practices to keep your project scope under control.
- **Stakeholder engagement:** Keep your stakeholders involved throughout the project lifecycle.
Their input is invaluable for defining scope, validating deliverables, and preventing scope creep.
- **Clear communication:** Communicate the scope, progress, and any changes to everyone involved.
- **Ongoing monitoring:** Don’t just set it and forget it.
Monitor progress regularly, track changes, and promptly address any scope creep attempts.

### What is Scope Management in Agile Project Managementbvop.org › learn › scopemanagement
Project managers typically manage the scope, but program managers, project directors, and sponsors may have the final say.
The process of managing project scope involves analyzing change requests, documenting them, seeking formal approval or rejection from senior management, implementing changes, and redefining the scope.
Agile Scope Management suggests that the scope of a project should not be considered fixed and unchangeable, as this may cause missed opportunities to increase business value.
Unlike Waterfall management practices, Agile thinking views change as an opportunity rather than a problem.
> [!cite]- Source · *Jan 8, 2020*> [What is Scope Management in Agile Project Managementbvop.org › learn › scopemanagement](https://bvop.org/learn/scopemanagement/)

### How to prevent scope creep when managing a project from home
- **Learn how to say “no”:** A lot of scope creep comes from users or stakeholders who seem to think that developers are automatons.
A firm but gentle “no” followed by an explanation of your workload and technical limitations will help you push back on unreasonable requests that could lead to scope creep.
- **Provide extra lead time on tasks:** This will help protect against scope creep and ensure that tasks get done on time, even if your team members have to juggle work and household chores.
> [!cite]- Source · *May 26, 2021*> [How to prevent scope creep when managing a project from home](https://stackoverflow.blog/2021/05/27/how-to-prevent-scope-creep-when-managing-a-project-from-home/)

### How to effectively scope your software projects
- **Divide the project into small tasks, each taking two days or less**.
When you have tasks that are scoped to “**roughly 1 week,**” they often end up taking longer because you didn’t enumerate all the subtasks that you might need to do.
- Define **measurable milestones** to get to the project goal.
Schedule each with a specific calendar date representing when you expect this milestone to be reached.
Then, establish some sort of external accountability on these milestones by, for example, committing them to your manager and setting up milestone checkins.
…
- **Use historical data.** Keep track of whether you’ve tended to overscope or underscope projects in the past (most people tend to underscope).
Adjust your scoping accordingly.
- Keep in mind that **2X the number of people does not mean 1/2X the dev time**, as a result of communication overheads, ramp up time, etc.
